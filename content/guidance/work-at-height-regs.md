## What it covers

The Work at Height Regulations 2005 (WAHR) apply to all work where a person could fall a distance liable to cause injury. For scaffolding, the parts that matter most day to day are:

- **Regulation 4** - work at height must be properly planned, appropriately supervised and carried out in a safe manner.
- **Regulation 5** - everyone involved must be competent (see [[cisrs-competence]]).
- **Regulation 8 and Schedule 3 (Part 2)** - requirements for scaffolding, including strength and stability calculations *unless* the scaffold is assembled in a generally recognised standard configuration (in practice, [[tg20]] compliance or a manufacturer's standard configuration).
- **Schedule 3** also requires a plan for assembly, use and dismantling, drawn up by a competent person.
- **Regulation 12 and Schedule 7** - inspection and reports (see [[inspection-regime]]).

## What it means on site

- No scaffold is handed over without a handover inspection and certificate - [[handover-procedure]].
- If it isn't a recognised standard configuration, it needs a design - [[design-brief-checklist]].
- Collective protection (guardrails, advance guardrail systems) comes before personal fall protection - [[sg4]].
