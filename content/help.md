## What this is

The hub brings everything the company knows about scaffolding into one place:

| Section | What's in it |
|---|---|
| [Scaffold systems](#/systems) | Every scaffold type: when to use it, key requirements, common pitfalls, and where we've used it before |
| [Standards & procedures](#/guidance) | Legislation, industry guidance (TG20, SG4, TG4...), British Standards and company procedures, summarised for site |
| [Projects](#/projects) | Past, live and tendering jobs: systems used, key documents, and **lessons learned** |
| [Scaffold Advisor](#/advisor) | Describe a job and get a recommended scaffold, alternatives and what else you'll need |

It works on a computer, tablet or phone, and keeps working without an internet connection once the page has loaded.

> **Important:** This is a prototype. The project records are **sample data**, and the technical summaries must be checked by a competent person against the current edition of each standard before anyone relies on them.

## 1. Search for anything

The search box at the top of every page searches titles, facts and the full text of every page.

- Press the **/** key from anywhere to jump into search.
- Search by **problem**, not just by name. For example [no ground](#/search?q=no%20ground), [ties](#/search?q=ties), [occupied](#/search?q=occupied) or [highway licence](#/search?q=highway%20licence).
- Search by **place or client**, e.g. [Hackney](#/search?q=hackney).
- Press **Enter** to open the top result straight away. Press **Esc** to clear the search.

## 2. Browse and filter

Use **Systems**, **Standards** and **Projects** in the top menu.

- **Systems** can be filtered by category, or by the *need* they solve (e.g. "no ground bearing", "weather protection").
- **Standards** can be filtered by type (legislation, industry guidance, company procedure...).
- **Projects** can be filtered by status (Tendering, Live, Completed), borough, and the scaffold system used, e.g. "every job where we used a temporary roof".
- The filter box narrows the list as you type.

The address bar updates as you filter, so you can copy the link and send someone the exact filtered list.

## 3. Ask the Scaffold Advisor

Open **Advisor** in the menu, or the 💬 **Scaffold Advisor** button at the bottom of any page.

**Step 1: describe the job in a sentence.** Include:

- **what the work is:** re-roof, brickwork, windows, ceiling, bridge soffit, maintenance...
- **what the building is like:** Victorian terrace, 16-storey new build, church, warehouse...
- **anything awkward:** busy pavement, residents living in it, over a road, no room at ground level, listed...

For example:

- *Re-roof of a Victorian terrace on a busy street, residents staying in*
- *Stone repairs on the upper floors of a city office - the pavement must stay clear*
- *Repoint the brick arch soffit of a railway bridge over a road*
- *Change the light fittings in a school hall, two days*

**Step 2: answer any follow-up questions.** The advisor only asks about things it couldn't work out, usually one or two questions. Tap an answer, choose **Not sure / skip**, or tap **Just give me the answer**.

**Step 3: read the recommendation.** You'll see:

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Recommended** | The best-fit scaffold, and *why* it fits |
| **Also consider** | Other systems that could work |
| **You'll probably need alongside it** | Add-ons such as a temporary roof, loading bay, protection fan or stair tower. *Likely needed* or *Consider* shows how strong the case is |
| **Ruled out** | Systems that don't suit this job, and the reason |
| **Read before you design or price it** | The standards and procedures that apply |
| **Similar past jobs** | Our previous projects to learn from |

**Step 4: refine it.** Type extra details at any time (e.g. *"it's a closed site"* or *"about 40 m high"*), and the advice updates. The **What I've understood** panel shows the details the advisor is using. Click one to remove it if it got something wrong. **Start a new job** clears everything.

> **Warning:** The advisor gives a starting point based on the hub's rules. It is not a design. A competent scaffold designer must confirm the solution, and anything marked *bespoke design* needs a design brief ([[design-brief-checklist]]).

**Tip:** every link in an answer opens the full page, and your conversation is still there when you come back to the Advisor.

## 4. Read a page

Every system, standard and project page has the same layout:

- **The write-up** on the left: when to use it, requirements, pitfalls, checklists. Checklist boxes can be ticked on a tablet on site. They reset when the page is reloaded.
- **Key facts** on the right: category, design basis, owner, and the review dates.
- **Connected:** everything linked to this page, in both directions. A system page automatically lists every project that used it.
- **Review badges:** *Up to date*, *Review due soon* or *Review overdue*. Every page has an owner and a review date, so you can tell whether it's current.

Buttons at the bottom of each page:

- **Print / PDF:** a clean printout for site or a tender pack.
- **Copy link:** share the exact page.
- **Suggest an edit:** propose a correction (see below).

## 5. Use past projects for new work

Before pricing or designing a new job:

1. Ask the **Advisor**, or filter **Projects** by the system or borough.
2. Open the closest past jobs and read **Lessons learned**, for example the [Hackney re-roof](#/project/victorian-terrace-reroof-hackney), where the highway licence took 6 weeks, not 3.
3. Reuse the document references (design numbers, certificates) as a starting point.

## 6. Keep it up to date

The hub only stays useful if people correct it. There are two ways:

- **Suggest an edit** on any page opens that page's text on GitHub. Make the change and submit it.
- **Report a problem or add a project** through the forms on GitHub (*Issues → New issue*). No technical knowledge needed.

What happens next is automatic:

1. The change is **checked** for missing information, broken links and wrong dates.
2. The page's **owner approves** it (Design Office for systems, SHEQ for standards, Contracts for projects).
3. The website **republishes itself** within a few minutes.
4. Every Monday, owners get a reminder listing any pages past their review date.
